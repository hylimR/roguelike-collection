# Konbini Empire

Run konbini across Tokyo in real time, each one in a street fight with the rival chain next door. Every day runs from 06:00 to midnight, and two and a half minutes at 1x. Passers-by walk the street, and those after something you stock pick your shop or the rival's by how appealing each is. Customers take an item off the shelf, queue at the till, and pay when a cashier, or you, rings them up. Every sale also hits the rival's takings (its HP). Each rival customer heals it. Drive it to zero and it closes for good; two days later a tougher chain moves in.

The rival fights back. It warns a few seconds ahead, then launches an attack:

- a flyer promoter in a happi coat in the chain's colors, or a parade by its costume mascot (Tanuki Mart's tanuki, Usagi Express's rabbit, Kitsune 24's fox, Oni Discount's ogre, MEGAMART's robot), that poaches your customers;
- trash bags dumped at your door;
- a price war;
- fake reviews, typed on the sidewalk at your door;
- shoplifters in a hood and mask;
- a blackout that stops the tills and puts your shop's lights, pole sign and floodlights out, dim in daylight and near dark at night, while the tripped board on the back wall flashes its red lamp;
- a hostile takeover by three suits who each grab 15% of your cash at the door.

Click a threat to chase it off. Click the till to ring up a waiting customer; fast ring-ups build a combo that multiplies XP. Click a shelf to restock it. Customers who find an empty shelf, a full queue or a slow till leave angry, and your rating drops.

Spend what you earn on staff:

- cashiers;
- stock clerks, who never touch tonight's bills;
- cleaners for spills and trash;
- a guard;
- a promoter on the sidewalk.

Each applicant has a trait, such as speedy, slacker, night owl or charming. The staff panel sets crew and applicants in sunken wells under colored role tags with open slots, beside a key to what each role does, each role shown by the staffer you'll see on the street on a plate in its color (the key sits under whichever list is shorter, so a full crew still fits), and colors each trait by whether it works faster, slower or has a special effect; a full role's applicants fade with a flat Full pill where the Hire button would be. Every level-up and every closed rival or milestone offers a card: skills, the five abilities (Flyer Blitz, Time Sale, Counter Campaign, Influencer Visit, Express Truck), and legendary rewards; each card shows its picture on a plate tinted by its rarity, under a tag starred one to four by rarity, and is edged all round in that colour, rare and rarer cards glowing with it (a new-store listing is edged in its district's colour). Expansions for each store add product lines (coffee, karaage and oden at the counter; beer, ice cream and daily goods on more floor; bento, cup noodles and manga on the most floor), knock through a wall, or buy a neon sign, second register, vending machine, gacha corner, ATM, bench, camera or delivery bike.

New stores open on Tokyo lots. Each lot's neighbourhood type sets its foot traffic, rent, what the locals buy and how tough a rival it draws. Each lot also has a quirk: corner lot, bargain lease, haunted unit, tourist hotspot, bus stop or school. Each lot on offer shows as a card: the neighbourhood type and quirk as tags, foot traffic and rent on a sunken panel, what the locals buy as product icons, and the rival as a tag in its chain's color. Rent rises the longer you hold a store. Rent and wages for the whole chain are due at midnight. If you're short, the bank forecloses your weakest store; with nothing left to take, the run ends. Street events (matsuri, heatwave, cold snap, TV feature, roadworks, health inspection) change a store's day. A heatwave bleaches the street by day, a warm haze under glare from the sky, mirages of the sky lying in pale sheens down the lanes, and shimmer rising off the road in broken, wavering ripples that fade as they climb (never inside your air-conditioned shop), and leaves the night its own blue. - a pin over the rival's roof carrying a cream plate: its mascot in a roundel ringed in the chain's colour breaking the plate's edge, its name on a pill in that colour over a gauge of its takings just as wide, notched at each quarter, with the next attack's warning hung under it; when an event or a longer product list makes the store card tall enough to cover it, the pin drops below the card on a shorter stem, or slides out to its left with the stem leaning back to the roof;
- a small store card in the top-right corner that is only as tall as its content: the day's street event as an amber tag beside the branch's name, the neighbourhood type in its colour and the lot's quirk as tags like its lot card's, rent, wages and the day's customers served and lost (green and red once there are any) on a sunken well, the rival as a tag in its chain's color with its tier over a bar of its takings, and one key per product showing its stock bar and restock price (click to restock), a full shelf a pale mint tile marked Full in green;
- store tabs, round ability keys (a locked one pressed into a well shaded under its rim, its picture embossed there in one tone with its detail kept, one recharging wiped round like a clock hand with its seconds left in orange), a speech bubble for news, always three lines tall so the row above it never jumps, dropping older news and then shrinking its type when the New card key narrows it, so a tip or the newest line is never cut off mid-sentence and a key hint like [P] wraps with the word before it rather than alone, an amber New card key, a fan of three cards with a red badge counting the picks waiting, beside the news when a level-up or reward is pending, and a dock of colored app keys (Staff, Expand, Store, City) along the bottom; a key that can't be used fades to a flat, paler shade of its own color.

The store card hides on the city map, where each of your branches stands on its lot as a pin, its number and name on an orange pill whose tail drops a stem to a disc on the ground, with the rival's mascot roundel beside it, the roundel's ring emptying to cream as its takings fall. The game fills any window shape: a wider (ultrawide) or taller window shows more of the street instead of black bars, and the 3D scene renders at the screen's own resolution.

Sound follows the street on screen: the door chime as customers walk in, coins on every sale, a scanner beep that climbs with your ring-up combo, a whoosh and a poof for chased-off threats, an alert before each attack. Level-ups, closed rivals and the nightly report get their own jingles. The music changes with the hour, like a clock radio: a morning, an afternoon, an evening and a night piece, crossfading as the day goes on.

**Run:** open `project.godot` in Godot 4.6 and press Play.

**Web build:** `python tools/web.py [path to godot]` exports a single-threaded Web build to `build/web/` (ignored by git). Its sound is mixed by the engine (`audio/general/default_playback_type.web` is Stream), because Godot's default web sample playback came out silent here. It needs the standard Godot editor; the .NET build can't export to the Web. When the web export template isn't installed, it fetches just that file out of Godot's template archive. `node tools/shot.mjs` serves the build to headless Chromium (Playwright, WebGL through SwiftShader, a few frames a second) and runs steps in order: `eval:` GDScript run against the main node, `wait:`, `key:`, `click:` and `shot:` (a screenshot). It loads the page with `?dev`, which is the only time the game exposes that hook. Use it to check the game's look on a machine with no GPU or display:

```bash
node tools/shot.mjs "key:Space" "eval:main.sim.time = 125.0" "wait:3" "shot:/tmp/night.png" "eval:main.toggle_city()" "wait:4" "shot:/tmp/map.png"
```

**Artifact:** `python tools/web.py [path to godot] --artifact` makes a release build in `build/artifact/` to publish as a Claude Artifact, with `tools/artifact.html` as the page. Artifacts serve only web file types, at up to 15 MB each, so the engine ships gzipped as `index.wasm` (the page unpacks it as it loads) and the game pack as `index.pck.wasm`. Publish `index.html` with `index.js`, the two `index.audio*.worklet.js` files, `index.wasm` and `index.pck.wasm`.

**Controls:** mouse and keyboard (on a touch screen, taps act as clicks).

- **Click:** threats, the till and shelves. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom.
- **Space:** pause.
- **F:** speed (1x, 2x, 3x).
- **R:** ring up.
- **1-5:** abilities.
- **P:** open a pending card.
- **S:** staff.
- **G:** expand this store.
- **N:** new store.
- **M:** city map; right-drag turns it a quarter at a time.
- **V:** sound: on, music off, muted.
- **Tab:** next store.
- **Esc:** close.

The game opens on an intro card: your district and the rival as colored tags, then six ways to play on sunken tiles with pictures (ring up, chase off, restock, grow, branch out, pay the bills) and the keys as key caps. Cards, the staff panel and the nightly report pause the game, and their wrapped text sets at one tight leading throughout. The nightly report reads like a receipt: each store's row on a sunken card, the store numbered on a pill in its district's colour as on its tab and its rival's takings as a bar in the chain's color beside the chain's mascot roundel, then a till roll from midnight cash through interest and bills to the cash left, with the day's change on a green or red tag, beside tomorrow's bills on a tile of their own, coins on a plate that turns green when you can cover them and red when you can't. When the bills bounce, the run ends on a bankrupt card: the run's days, revenue, customers, rivals closed, threats repelled and stores opened on sunken tiles, each with its picture on a rose plate (a lantern, coins, a till, a rival konbini, a flyer, your konbini), and the score with a green tag for a new best. The seed button opens a new-run card: this run's seed, day and level on a sunken tile with your konbini on a mint plate and a button to replay it, beside a tile with a gacha machine on its plate and a field for a new seed (blank for random). The New store card shows each lot on offer on a plate in its district's colour, your konbini facing the rival chain's shopfront, over its tags, traffic, rent, what locals buy and the rival next door.

**Check:** `godot --headless --path games/konbini-empire --script res://tests/balance.gd`. It checks:

- the random generator against reference vectors;
- the XP, expansion, reroll, store-price, rent-age and rival-HP curves;
- appeal values and listing prices;
- the starting state;
- that a rejected action leaves the seed alone.

A bot then plays eight days on two seeds, asserting invariants every step and that a seed replays identically.

`godot --headless --path games/konbini-empire --script res://tests/audio.gd` checks that every sound the game plays exists and loads, and that every hour of the day has music.

**Code:**

- `sim.gd` holds the rules: no nodes, and positions in a street frame of 1 m units, with your door at x = -5.4 and the rival's at x = 14.
- `city.gd` lays out Tokyo; it is built once and cached.
- `store_view.gd` draws the city and each branch's street set, and turns clicks into actions.
- `main.gd` holds the HUD, the cards and the real-time loop.
- `audio.gd` plays the effects and the time-of-day music on their own buses.

**Art:** sprites and backgrounds were generated by Codex image_gen on a magenta key, then cut and downsampled into `art/`. Buildings and shop fittings are drawn in code, in one house style: every box takes three flat tones (roof full, walls turned to the sun 0.9, walls away 0.75, the drawn art's ratio between its lit and shaded walls), and on the city map each branch's shop and its rival are the same boxes the street set builds, not pictures. Their colours come from palettes in `city.gd` that the city map and the street share: wall tints by building style (sky and aqua towers, sand and terracotta offices, pastel flats, shops and houses, jewel-toned neon blocks, red-brown timber, painted tin sheds), glazed blue, green, terracotta and amber gable tiles, flat roofs coated green, teal or terracotta and taking 30% of their walls' tint, and each district type's ground, on clay pavers and slate tarmac among green fields and paddies, laid in farm blocks between gravel lanes, each block cut into strips one way and the strips into fields furrowed down their length; `tests/balance.gd` checks that the walls, roofs and ground the city builds are tinted and spread round the colour wheel, not beige and grey. Fittings with a front (the till, coffee machine, hot-snack case, oden pot, ATM, vending machine, gacha row and bench) are in `art/props.png`, drawn in the store camera's own 2:1 view: each was blocked out in boxes at real size and rendered from that camera, then Codex painted over the blockout, so the art lands exactly where the boxes would. They stand on billboards scaled up by 1/cos 30° (the art already is the tilted view) and slid toward the camera along its line of sight, which keeps their place on screen while their lower edge clears the floor. Front-on art of a boxy object on a billboard reads turned 45° off the counter or wall behind it, so new fittings with a front get the same treatment. People, trees, round things and icons stay front-on billboards. The people sheet `art/walk.png` holds a row per character (a standing frame, then four of a walk cycle): the seven customers, then each staff role and threat in its own outfit, a flyer girl per rival chain (one Codex drawing, its cyan coat recoloured per chain) and each chain's mascot. Every city-map landmark is built in code too, traced from its 2:1 drawing into `landmark_models.gd` by `tools/trace/trace.py`: its `render.py` draws a model of boxes, gables, cylinders, domes, barrel vaults, beams, temple roofs and arched walls in the city camera's own projection and scores it against the drawing, Codex edits the model until it matches, and two blind reviewers compare the result with the drawing in the game before it is kept. The game builds each model with the city's buildings: their shader and three tones, windows (a round wall with windows is built as turned wall panels), night lights and shadows; the curved parts arrive as the same flat faces `render.py` scored. Green crowns become the city's tree sprites, and a landmark's pools and lawns take the city's own textured water and park grass. Tokyo Tower and the Skytree are lattices of beams (Tokyo Tower floodlit amber after dark, a face colour's alpha setting its glow), the Imperial Palace's double bridge an arched wall over the moat. The drawings stay in `art/` as the trace's reference, and a landmark without a model would still show its drawing. A landmark stands on a 22 m square at the centre of a block near its district's heart; where the dense centre leaves none (Tokyo Station, Ginza, Akihabara, Asakusa, Ueno, Jimbocho), it takes the biggest square down to 14 m that fits anywhere in the district's own blocks (failing that, any block within 60 m; Odaiba's island, cut up by its roads and the Yurikamome, gets one reaching out over the water from its shore), and its model is scaled to it. Tokyo Station, drawn longer than any square, is built compressed into its own. Sound effects and music were generated with ElevenLabs (Sound Effects and Eleven Music) from text prompts. Fonts: Nunito, with Noto Sans and a three-glyph subset of Noto Sans Math (★ ♪ ▾) as fallbacks, so the web build needs no system fonts (all SIL Open Font License; Nunito's licence is in `fonts/OFL-Nunito.txt`).

# Konbini Empire

Run konbini across Tokyo in real time, each one in a street fight with the rival chain next door. Every day runs from 06:00 to midnight, and two and a half minutes at 1x. Passers-by walk the street, and those after something you stock pick your shop or the rival's by how appealing each is. Customers take an item off the shelf, queue at the till, and pay when a cashier, or you, rings them up. Every sale also hits the rival's takings (its HP). Each rival customer heals it. Drive it to zero and it closes for good; two days later a tougher chain moves in.

The rival fights back. It warns a few seconds ahead, then launches an attack:

- a flyer promoter in a happi coat in the chain's colors, or a parade by its costume mascot (Tanuki Mart's tanuki, Usagi Express's rabbit, Kitsune 24's fox, Oni Discount's ogre, MEGAMART's robot), that poaches your customers;
- trash bags dumped at your door;
- a price war;
- fake reviews, typed on the sidewalk at your door;
- shoplifters in a hood and mask;
- a blackout that stops the tills and puts your shop's lights, pole sign and floodlights out, dim in daylight and near dark at night, while the tripped board on the back wall flashes its red lamp;
- a hostile takeover by three suits who each grab 15% of your cash at the door.

Click a threat to chase it off. Click the till to ring up a waiting customer; fast ring-ups build a combo that multiplies XP. Click a shelf to restock it. Customers who find an empty shelf, a full queue or a slow till leave angry, and your rating drops.

Spend what you earn on staff:

- cashiers;
- stock clerks, who never touch tonight's bills;
- cleaners for spills and trash;
- a guard;
- a promoter on the sidewalk.

Each applicant has a trait, such as speedy, slacker, night owl or charming. The staff panel sets crew and applicants in sunken wells under colored role tags with open slots, beside a key to what each role does, each role shown by the staffer you'll see on the street on a plate in its color (the key sits under whichever list is shorter, so a full crew still fits), and colors each trait by whether it works faster, slower or has a special effect; a full role's applicants fade with a flat Full pill where the Hire button would be. Every level-up and every closed rival or milestone offers a card: skills, the five abilities (Flyer Blitz, Time Sale, Counter Campaign, Influencer Visit, Express Truck), and legendary rewards; each card shows its picture on a plate tinted by its rarity. Expansions for each store add product lines (coffee, karaage and oden at the counter; beer, ice cream and daily goods on more floor; bento, cup noodles and manga on the most floor), knock through a wall, or buy a neon sign, second register, vending machine, gacha corner, ATM, bench, camera or delivery bike.

New stores open on Tokyo lots. Each lot's neighbourhood type sets its foot traffic, rent, what the locals buy and how tough a rival it draws. Each lot also has a quirk: corner lot, bargain lease, haunted unit, tourist hotspot, bus stop or school. Each lot on offer shows as a card: the neighbourhood type and quirk as tags, foot traffic and rent on a sunken panel, what the locals buy as product icons, and the rival as a tag in its chain's color. Rent rises the longer you hold a store. Rent and wages for the whole chain are due at midnight. If you're short, the bank forecloses your weakest store; with nothing left to take, the run ends. Street events (matsuri, heatwave, cold snap, TV feature, roadworks, health inspection) change a store's day. A heatwave bleaches the street by day, a warm haze under glare from the sky with shimmer rising off the tarmac (never inside your air-conditioned shop), and leaves the night its own blue. A kakigōri stand sets up in your free bay under a blue and white parasol, the red shaved-ice machine and syrups on its table, a cool box of drinks on ice beside it and a 氷 flag at the kerb, and the sidewalk at your door is splashed dark against the heat. The cold snap's snow shows on the street too: flakes falling at three depths, snow lying on the sidewalks, the car park and every roof, flat or pitched (but never on the cut-down rooms in front of the street), and slush along the kerbs and between the road's dark wheel tracks. On a matsuri, red and white paper lanterns hang on ropes along both kerbs, kept clear of your shopfront, and glow after dark, and a yakisoba yatai stands in your free bay under a red and white striped canopy, noodles on its griddle and red lanterns at its corners, throwing warm light on the bay at night. Roadworks fence off the near kerb lane with striped barricades and cones over steel plates and an open trench, an arrow board turning traffic out, their red lamps and arrow flashing after dark. A TV feature brings a broadcast van in the station's livery, a dish on its roof, into your car park, with a camera on a tripod at the kerb and a soft light by your shopfront, the camera's red tally and the light glowing after dark. On a health inspection the ward health centre's white wagon, a green band round it and a green cross on its roof sign that lights up after dark, parks in your car park, and a sanitiser stand waits by your door.

The city is 48 real neighbourhoods laid out roughly where they are:

- Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ikebukuro, Ueno and Tokyo Station as Yamanote Line hubs;
- Ginza and Azabu for luxury;
- Roppongi and Ebisu for nightlife;
- Akihabara for otaku;
- Asakusa and Odaiba for tourists;
- Sugamo's grandmothers and Tsukiji's early workers;
- the Imperial Palace in the middle.

It's drawn like a city builder:

- Blocks split into side streets that meet in T-junctions and corners, and curved avenues cut across them. Every road ends on another road: a street runs exactly along the blocks it serves, bridges the Sumida, stops at a railed quay on the bay or the palace moat, and meets the ring road round each park; an avenue runs on to the next road. `tests/balance.gd` checks every end.
- The elevated Shuto expressway rings the palace and runs out along the radial avenues, with ramps down to the street: each ramp eases level into the deck's outer lanes at the top and lands in the avenue's outer lane at the foot, routes open into the ring where they join it, and the Rainbow Bridge comes down onto Odaiba's street. A parapet or painted line is left out wherever another carriageway runs into its own (`tests/balance.gd` checks every ramp, deck end and line).
- The Rainbow Bridge crosses to Odaiba. The Yamanote loop runs on a double-track viaduct, its tracks laid as steel rails on concrete sleepers over speckled ballast, straight between its twelve stations and round every corner on a curve of at least 30 m, clear of the parks and the moat, under the expressway (raised to clear a train) and never through its pillars. Four-car trains run both ways, keeping left, and stop at an island platform under a canopy at every station; their windows light up after dark. The viaduct and the expressway are never cut away, in the shop view either: the city keeps every shop lot where the set the shop view builds round it (your shop, car park, pole sign and the rival next door) stands clear of them, none of their piers or pillars in it and none in front of your shop, and the set's own rows of neighbours, utility poles and street lamps keep out from under them. `tests/balance.gd` checks the line, its piers, its stations, the trains' spacing and every shop's set.
- Every block is packed with 3D buildings drawn by a pixel facade shader: glass towers at the hubs, neon in the nightlife districts, wooden machiya in the old town, gable-roofed houses in the suburbs. Flat roofs sit inside a parapet whose shadow falls across the roof on its sunny sides, laid in membrane strips with a stair hatch.
- Open ground between buildings is paved in metre slabs, each row set half a slab along, a shade apart with a few weathered darker, or fine gravel in the leafy districts, so a station plaza reads as a square rather than bare ground; the bay islands and the made ground a waterfront street stands on are paved the same, rather than a bare tan sheet.
- Beyond the built-up edge, homes thin out into a whole patchwork of furrowed fields and woods, mostly green, neighbouring fields sharing a crop in its own shade so they lie in broad patches rather than a checkerboard (after dark the crops fade into one dark green, leaving the lit city to stand out), and the bay opens out to the south, its water (and the rivers') mottled in broad patches and scattered with short wave crests that glint in and out, the same size on screen at any zoom; both run on past the frame at any zoom or pan, so the map never shows its edge.

- a small store card in the top-right corner that is only as tall as its content: the day's street event as an amber tag beside the branch's name, the neighbourhood type in its colour and the lot's quirk as tags like its lot card's, rent, wages and the day's customers served and lost (green and red once there are any) on a sunken well, the rival as a tag in its chain's color with its tier over a bar of its takings, and one key per product showing its stock bar and restock price (click to restock), a full shelf a pale mint tile marked Full in green;
- store tabs, round ability keys (a locked one sunk into a well with its picture in silhouette, one recharging wiped round like a clock hand with its seconds left in orange), a speech bubble for news, always three lines tall so the row above it never jumps, dropping older news and then shrinking its type when the New card key narrows it, so a tip or the newest line is never cut off mid-sentence, an amber New card key, a fan of three cards with a red badge counting the picks waiting, beside the news when a level-up or reward is pending, and a dock of colored app keys (Staff, Expand, Store, City) along the bottom; a key that can't be used fades to a flat, paler shade of its own color.

The store card hides on the city map. The game fills any window shape: a wider (ultrawide) or taller window shows more of the street instead of black bars, and the 3D scene renders at the screen's own resolution.

Sound follows the street on screen: the door chime as customers walk in, coins on every sale, a scanner beep that climbs with your ring-up combo, a whoosh and a poof for chased-off threats, an alert before each attack. Level-ups, closed rivals and the nightly report get their own jingles. The music changes with the hour, like a clock radio: a morning, an afternoon, an evening and a night piece, crossfading as the day goes on.

**Run:** open `project.godot` in Godot 4.6 and press Play.

**Web build:** `python tools/web.py [path to godot]` exports a single-threaded Web build to `build/web/` (ignored by git). Its sound is mixed by the engine (`audio/general/default_playback_type.web` is Stream), because Godot's default web sample playback came out silent here. It needs the standard Godot editor; the .NET build can't export to the Web. When the web export template isn't installed, it fetches just that file out of Godot's template archive. `node tools/shot.mjs` serves the build to headless Chromium (Playwright, WebGL through SwiftShader, a few frames a second) and runs steps in order: `eval:` GDScript run against the main node, `wait:`, `key:`, `click:` and `shot:` (a screenshot). It loads the page with `?dev`, which is the only time the game exposes that hook. Use it to check the game's look on a machine with no GPU or display:

```bash
node tools/shot.mjs "key:Space" "eval:main.sim.time = 125.0" "wait:3" "shot:/tmp/night.png" "eval:main.toggle_city()" "wait:4" "shot:/tmp/map.png"
```

**Artifact:** `python tools/web.py [path to godot] --artifact` makes a release build in `build/artifact/` to publish as a Claude Artifact, with `tools/artifact.html` as the page. Artifacts serve only web file types, at up to 15 MB each, so the engine ships gzipped as `index.wasm` (the page unpacks it as it loads) and the game pack as `index.pck.wasm`. Publish `index.html` with `index.js`, the two `index.audio*.worklet.js` files, `index.wasm` and `index.pck.wasm`.

**Controls:** mouse and keyboard (on a touch screen, taps act as clicks).

- **Click:** threats, the till and shelves. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom.
- **Space:** pause.
- **F:** speed (1x, 2x, 3x).
- **R:** ring up.
- **1-5:** abilities.
- **P:** open a pending card.
- **S:** staff.
- **G:** expand this store.
- **N:** new store.
- **M:** city map; right-drag turns it a quarter at a time.
- **V:** sound: on, music off, muted.
- **Tab:** next store.
- **Esc:** close.

The game opens on an intro card: your district and the rival as colored tags, then six ways to play on sunken tiles with pictures (ring up, chase off, restock, grow, branch out, pay the bills) and the keys as key caps. Cards, the staff panel and the nightly report pause the game, and their wrapped text sets at one tight leading throughout. The nightly report reads like a receipt: each store's row on a sunken card with its rival's takings as a bar in the chain's color, then a till roll from midnight cash through interest and bills to the cash left, with the day's change on a green or red tag, beside tomorrow's bills on a tile of their own, coins on a plate that turns green when you can cover them and red when you can't. When the bills bounce, the run ends on a bankrupt card: the run's days, revenue, customers, rivals closed, threats repelled and stores opened on sunken tiles, each with its picture on a rose plate (a lantern, coins, a till, a rival konbini, a flyer, your konbini), and the score with a green tag for a new best. The seed button opens a new-run card: this run's seed, day and level on a sunken tile with your konbini on a mint plate and a button to replay it, beside a tile with a gacha machine on its plate and a field for a new seed (blank for random). The New store card shows each lot on offer on a plate in its district's colour, your konbini facing the rival chain's shopfront, over its tags, traffic, rent, what locals buy and the rival next door.

**Check:** `godot --headless --path games/konbini-empire --script res://tests/balance.gd`. It checks:

- the random generator against reference vectors;
- the XP, expansion, reroll, store-price, rent-age and rival-HP curves;
- appeal values and listing prices;
- the starting state;
- that a rejected action leaves the seed alone.

A bot then plays eight days on two seeds, asserting invariants every step and that a seed replays identically.

`godot --headless --path games/konbini-empire --script res://tests/audio.gd` checks that every sound the game plays exists and loads, and that every hour of the day has music.

**Code:**

- `sim.gd` holds the rules: no nodes, and positions in a street frame of 1 m units, with your door at x = -5.4 and the rival's at x = 14.
- `city.gd` lays out Tokyo; it is built once and cached.
- `store_view.gd` draws the city and each branch's street set, and turns clicks into actions.
- `main.gd` holds the HUD, the cards and the real-time loop.
- `audio.gd` plays the effects and the time-of-day music on their own buses.

**Art:** sprites and backgrounds were generated by Codex image_gen on a magenta key, then cut and downsampled into `art/`. Buildings and shop fittings are drawn in code, in one house style: every box takes three flat tones (roof full, walls turned to the sun 0.9, walls away 0.75, the drawn art's ratio between its lit and shaded walls), and on the city map each branch's shop and its rival are the same boxes the street set builds, not pictures. Their colours come from palettes in `city.gd` that the city map and the street share: wall tints by building style (sky and aqua towers, sand and terracotta offices, pastel flats, shops and houses, jewel-toned neon blocks, red-brown timber, painted tin sheds), glazed blue, green, terracotta and amber gable tiles, flat roofs coated green, teal or terracotta and taking 30% of their walls' tint, and each district type's ground, on clay pavers and slate tarmac among green fields and paddies; `tests/balance.gd` checks that the walls, roofs and ground the city builds are tinted and spread round the colour wheel, not beige and grey. Fittings with a front (the till, coffee machine, hot-snack case, oden pot, ATM, vending machine, gacha row and bench) are in `art/props.png`, drawn in the store camera's own 2:1 view: each was blocked out in boxes at real size and rendered from that camera, then Codex painted over the blockout, so the art lands exactly where the boxes would. They stand on billboards scaled up by 1/cos 30° (the art already is the tilted view) and slid toward the camera along its line of sight, which keeps their place on screen while their lower edge clears the floor. Front-on art of a boxy object on a billboard reads turned 45° off the counter or wall behind it, so new fittings with a front get the same treatment. People, trees, round things and icons stay front-on billboards. The people sheet `art/walk.png` holds a row per character (a standing frame, then four of a walk cycle): the seven customers, then each staff role and threat in its own outfit, a flyer girl per rival chain (one Codex drawing, its cyan coat recoloured per chain) and each chain's mascot. Every city-map landmark is built in code too, traced from its 2:1 drawing into `landmark_models.gd` by `tools/trace/trace.py`: its `render.py` draws a model of boxes, gables, cylinders, domes, barrel vaults, beams, temple roofs and arched walls in the city camera's own projection and scores it against the drawing, Codex edits the model until it matches, and two blind reviewers compare the result with the drawing in the game before it is kept. The game builds each model with the city's buildings: their shader and three tones, windows (a round wall with windows is built as turned wall panels), night lights and shadows; the curved parts arrive as the same flat faces `render.py` scored. Green crowns become the city's tree sprites, and a landmark's pools and lawns take the city's own textured water and park grass. Tokyo Tower and the Skytree are lattices of beams (Tokyo Tower floodlit amber after dark, a face colour's alpha setting its glow), the Imperial Palace's double bridge an arched wall over the moat. The drawings stay in `art/` as the trace's reference, and a landmark without a model would still show its drawing. A landmark stands on a 22 m square at the centre of a block near its district's heart; where the dense centre leaves none (Tokyo Station, Ginza, Akihabara, Asakusa, Ueno, Jimbocho), it takes the biggest square down to 14 m that fits anywhere in the district's own blocks (failing that, any block within 60 m; Odaiba's island, cut up by its roads and the Yurikamome, gets one reaching out over the water from its shore), and its model is scaled to it. Tokyo Station, drawn longer than any square, is built compressed into its own. Sound effects and music were generated with ElevenLabs (Sound Effects and Eleven Music) from text prompts. Fonts: Nunito, with Noto Sans and a three-glyph subset of Noto Sans Math (★ ♪ ▾) as fallbacks, so the web build needs no system fonts (all SIL Open Font License; Nunito's licence is in `fonts/OFL-Nunito.txt`).
